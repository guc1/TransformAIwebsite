# Welcome to TransformAI: An Invitation, Not a Manual

Welcome—and congratulations on finding this first post. It won’t hand you a tidy framework or a checklist for AI transformations. Think of it instead as the foyer before the real rooms: a place to hang your coat, look around, and decide if you want to step further in. Still, there’s a small gift tucked inside; if you read carefully, you’ll find it.

TransformAI (formerly StaccatoAI) began in early 2025 with a simple mission: help companies—especially in the Netherlands—prepare for what may be one of the most transformative periods in human history: the age of AI. I’m not here to be dramatic, and I enjoy good debate, but the balance of research points in one direction: AI will reshape society. I’m convinced it can do so for the better. That doesn’t make the work easy. Transformation rarely is—least of all for companies. But it is necessary, and “later” quietly becomes “never” more often than we admit.

Before TransformAI, I worked in consulting. I learned a lot, and also learned what I didn’t want to recreate. Many agencies are engineered to optimize billable hours, not durable capability. That’s why we don’t call ourselves a consultancy. We prefer the word partner. It’s not branding; it’s stance. A partner shows up differently: curious, honest about tradeoffs, and willing to teach you everything we know—even if that means you won’t need us one day.

Teaching, in fact, is the heart of what we do. The greatest long-term value—for you and for us—lives in your ability to navigate AI yourselves. AI is not a single switch to flip; it’s millions of small and large shifts that arrive in waves, overlapping and accelerating. No one memo can capture it. No one model release will settle it. The work is learning to integrate, to adapt, to keep your hands on the wheel as the road keeps redrawing itself.

If you’re expecting certainty, you won’t find it here. What you will find is resolve. We believe businesses should transform AI into their business, not let AI transform their business. The distinction matters. It’s about agency. It’s about refusing the passive voice—“was transformed”—and insisting on the active: we chose, we learned, we built, we changed. It’s about aligning tools to purpose, not contorting purpose to fit tools.

You might wonder where to begin. Honestly, anywhere you are willing to pay attention. Attention, after all, is the scarcest resource in any transformation: the quiet discipline of noticing what actually happens when a process changes; the humility to name where things break; the energy to fix them; the patience to try again. Most “AI failures” are attention failures wearing a technical mask.

And because you’ve made it this far, here’s the promised surprise. Tell us the word **attentionblocks**—email it to us or mention it on a call—and we’ll have something special for you. It won’t change the world. But it might make the first step easier.

If you decide to keep reading this blog, you’ll see essays that tilt practical but don’t rush past the human questions. We’ll share what we’re learning as we help teams build capability. We’ll resist the urge to inflate. We’ll try to be precise where it matters and plainspoken everywhere else. Some posts will be closer to the whiteboard; others will be closer to the workshop floor. All will be oriented toward one thing: helping you hold the pen as your organization writes its next chapter.

For now, let this serve as an introduction—less a thesis than an invitation. AI is here to stay. The choice is not whether to engage, but how. You can be dragged by it, or you can meet it with a plan, a partner, and a willingness to learn in public.

Nice to meet you. I’m Yergush, founder of TransformAI. Are you ready? I am.
