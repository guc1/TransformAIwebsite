# Winning the Model: Ranking Higher in AI Recommendations

![Homescreen preview](/images/blog-images/mdxfilesforprojects/research/Chatbotranking/images/screenshotofchat.png)

Over the last year, purchases made via ChatGPT-like assistants have grown by orders of magnitude. People ask AI what to buy, which service to choose, and which plan fits their needs. The assistant hasn’t used those products; it reasons from training data, any retrieved evidence, and the prompt. That makes **visibility inside model outputs** a new growth channel—akin to SEO for Google, but fundamentally different.

This post explains why “Model Optimization” matters, how it differs from classic SEO, what tends to influence inclusion and prominence, and what our early benchmarks show.

## Why “ranking” in AI models matters

When a user asks, “What’s the best X for Y?”, the model synthesizes an answer from:
- Pretraining priors (what it already “knows”)
- Retrieval or browsing (what it can find now)
- Prompt constraints (format, guardrails, scope)
- Internal tendencies (safety, diversity, deduplication)

If your brand isn’t represented in those ingredients—or isn’t easy for the model to cite confidently—you’re unlikely to appear. If you *are* represented in ways that align with selection criteria, you tend to show up more often and higher.

## It’s like SEO, but not SEO

Similarities:
- Competition for **inclusion and prominence**
- Emphasis on **high-quality, structured evidence**
- Continuous **measurement and iteration**

Key differences:
- **Single-shot synthesis**: No results page to scroll; exclusion often means invisibility.
- **Citation confidence** over backlinks: Models reward content that’s clear, consistent, and machine-parseable.
- **Policy & safety filters**: Provider-specific heuristics influence what’s eligible to appear.
- **Cross-model variance**: Different providers weigh signals differently.

## What influences model outputs (aggregate view)

Our research evaluates, in aggregate, how these factors correlate with inclusion and position across providers:

- **Evidence surfaces**: The presence, clarity, and depth of product/service pages, specs, pricing, reviews, FAQs, comparisons, and docs.  
- **Structure & clarity**: Clean headings, canonical claims, consistent names/SKUs, versions, and units that models can parse reliably.  
- **Third-party corroboration**: Independent sources that restate key facts and reduce uncertainty for the model.  
- **Prompt-resilience**: Stability of facts across different phrasings, languages, and specificity levels.  
- **Policy alignment**: Safety cues, disclaimers, support transparency, and handling of regulated categories.

## Benchmark setup (high level)

- 150+ queries across 6 product/service categories  
- Leading models from multiple providers, tested with and without retrieval/browse  
- Metrics tracked: **inclusion rate**, **average position**, and **share of voice** per query cluster

## Early results (directional)

![Relative lift overview](/images/blog-images/mdxfilesforprojects/research/Chatbotranking/images/Chartofranking.png)

Across categories in our testbed, emphasizing the factors above produced substantial lifts in inclusion and visibility relative to baselines—**on the order of ~72%, 89%, and 75%**, varying by domain, competition, and provider settings.

> Note: Percentages reflect relative lift versus category baselines in our environment. Effects vary by market and model configuration.

## What’s next

We’re expanding the benchmark to more verticals and will publish provider-specific nuances where they’re stable and ethical to share. If you want a scoped assessment, we can run a light audit and report on inclusion, position, and the most impactful content gaps.

### TL;DR

- AI assistants are becoming **buying guides**; inclusion in their answers is a real growth lever.  
- It’s “SEO-like,” but models respond most to **clear, structured evidence, corroboration, and policy alignment**.  
- In our tests, those emphases yielded **~72%, 89%, and 75%** relative lifts in inclusion and visibility across models.
