# How We Map AI Adoption Across Industries

AI is moving from hype to habit. Every week, more teams put models into products, workflows, and decisions. But “adoption” isn’t a single switch—it has a speed, a direction, and a depth. Our research program exists to measure those three things, rigorously and repeatably, so leaders can see where their sector stands and what to do next.

![Research schematic](/images/blog-images/mdxfilesforprojects/research/ai-adoptation/images/insideblogpost.png)

## What we study

For each sector, we analyze four dimensions:

- **Fields of adoption** — Where AI is actually used (e.g., customer service, marketing ops, software delivery, risk, R&D).
- **Speed** — How quickly adoption spreads, from pilot to scale, measured over time.
- **Depth** — How integrated the change is: surface-level tooling vs. workflow redesign vs. core business model shifts.
- **Outcomes** — We always **try** to quantify what changed (e.g., “AHT down 27%,” “lead quality +14%”), grounding numbers in available evidence.

We don’t just look for trends—we also flag **anomalies** (both positive and negative) to capture the full picture: unexpected accelerations, outlier results, and early-warning signals where adoption outpaces capability or governance.

## Why it matters

“Using AI” can mean anything from trying a few prompts to rebuilding a value chain. Without a common lens, comparisons are meaningless. Our framework separates signal from noise:

- **Direction** shows *where* in the enterprise AI is gaining traction.
- **Speed** shows *how fast* momentum is building.
- **Depth** shows *how real* the change is beneath the surface.
- **Outcomes** show *whether it works*—and by how much.

## How we do it (in plain language)

We triangulate across many data points—public disclosures, job and skill signals, product updates, vendor footprints, case studies, and direct interviews—then standardize them into a sector scorecard:

1. **Map** common AI use-cases per sector and tag them to functions and workflows.
2. **Track** movement over time (pilots → production → scale) to calculate velocity.
3. **Assess** depth with a structured rubric (Tooling → Workflow → Systems → Strategy).
4. **Quantify** outcomes where we can—and **always try** to translate change into % terms or comparable metrics.
5. **Benchmark** sectors against peers and against their own past trajectory.
6. **Survey at scale** via two channels: targeted surveys with our network **and** AI-driven survey systems that collect and summarize responses automatically.
7. **Web-wide evidence mining** using AI systems that continuously scan for mentions of AI usage; we weight these signals and synthesize them into representative generalizations.
8. **Scientific discipline**: we apply transparent weighting, reproducible code, and clear rubrics to maximize **objectivity** and **repeatability**.

## Our depth rubric (at a glance)

- **Level 1 — Surface**: Add-on tools, no process change.
- **Level 2 — Embedded**: Workflows redesigned, handoffs automated.
- **Level 3 — Systemic**: Data and platforms re-architected around AI.
- **Level 4 — Transformational**: Products, pricing, or operating model materially change.

## What this unlocks

- **Prioritization** — See which use-cases in your sector actually produce measurable results, so you invest in the few that matter rather than spreading effort thin.
- **Sequencing** — Move intentionally from quick wins (Level 1–2) to durable, systemic gains (Level 3–4), with evidence-based next steps for data, process, and org design.
- **Risk control** — Spot gaps early (e.g., rapid adoption without model governance, security, or change management) and correct course before issues compound.
- **Communication** — Replace anecdotes with shared, comparable metrics that align boards, finance, and delivery teams on progress and value creation.
- **Anomaly awareness** — Learn from outliers: replicate positive deviations, and investigate negative ones to prevent wasted spend or unintended consequences.
